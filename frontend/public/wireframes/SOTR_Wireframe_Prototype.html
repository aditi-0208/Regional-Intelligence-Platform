Methodology

How we measure regional vitality — data sources, indicator selection, and scoring framework.

Our framework

We measure regional vitality across four equally-weighted pillars, each comprising 8–12 indicators drawn from publicly available federal datasets. Each pillar contributes 25% to the overall vitality score.

Economy
25% weight
Workforce
25% weight
Housing
25% weight
Innovation
25% weight
Framework: Pillars → Composite Score

Data sources

All indicators use publicly available federal datasets updated on a regular schedule. All data is fully reproducible from original sources.

SourceIndicators coveredUpdate frequencyPillar
BEA — Bureau of Economic AnalysisGDP, personal income, productivityAnnualEconomy
BLS — Bureau of Labor StatisticsEmployment, wages, job growthMonthly / AnnualEconomy, Workforce
ACS — American Community SurveyEducation, income, demographicsAnnualWorkforce, Housing
Census Bureau / HUDHousing costs, supply, affordabilityAnnualHousing
USPTOPatent filings by metroAnnualInnovation
NVCA / PitchbookVC investment, startup densityAnnualInnovation

How scores are calculated

Each indicator is normalized to a 0–100 scale relative to national performance. A score of 50 means exactly median national performance; above 50 means above average.

Pillar scores are the average of their constituent indicators. The overall vitality score is the equally weighted average of all four pillar scores.

Worked example
Tampa Bay scores 72 on Economy. This means it outperforms 72% of tracked metros on economic indicators — not that it achieved 72% of some absolute target.

Limitations & caveats

!Data lags: Most indicators use data from 12–18 months prior to publication. The 2025 report reflects conditions through late 2023 or early 2024.
!Metro boundaries: We use OMB-defined Metropolitan Statistical Areas, which may differ from how residents define their region.
!Equal weighting: Equal pillar weighting (25% each) is a deliberate methodological choice. Different weights would produce different rankings.
!Coverage: We track 150+ metros with populations over 250,000. Smaller regions are not included due to data reliability concerns.

About this project

Regional Macro-Economic Insights is a research initiative at the University of South Florida that tracks and publishes data on economic vitality across US metro regions. Our goal is to make regional data accessible, credible, and useful for decision-makers, researchers, and the public.

Background

This project began as part of a graduate research seminar focused on regional economic vitality in the post-pandemic era.

The platform was developed to fill a gap in publicly accessible, independently produced data on how US metro regions compare — bringing together federal datasets that are technically public but practically difficult to access and interpret.
